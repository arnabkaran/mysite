ABOUT ME

I am a PhD physicist specializing in materials science and optoelectronic devices, with a research background in condensed matter physics, photovoltaic devices, halide perovskites, organic dye-based devices, coordination polymers and semiconductor characterization.

Ph.D. Thesis Title

Influence of Nanoparticles and Carbon Nanotubes on Electrical Characteristics of Natural and Organic Dye-based Cell


Abstract

This research investigates the enhancement of charge transport and electrical performance in organic dye-based semiconducting devices using TiO₂ and ZnO nanoparticles and multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs). Brilliant Blue, Sunset Yellow, and Carmoisine-based devices were studied to understand the effects of nanomaterials on charge injection, barrier characteristics, activation energy, trap-assisted conduction, and hopping transport. The results demonstrate that TiO₂ improves interfacial uniformity, ZnO reduces activation energy and enhances carrier mobility, while MWCNTs create efficient conductive pathways and improve charge transport, highlighting nanomaterial engineering as an effective strategy for enhancing organic dye-based electronic devices.

Projects & Mentorship

1. To design and develop a low-cost Arduino-based digital clock capable of measuring and displaying elapsed time with millisecond resolution using microcontroller timer functions and a digital display interface.

An Arduino-Based Digital Millisecond Clock is an embedded system designed to measure and display time with millisecond-level resolution. It uses an Arduino microcontroller, digital display, and control buttons to accurately track elapsed time. The project demonstrates microcontroller programming, digital electronics, timer operation, real-time processing, and human machine interfacing, making it useful for educational and experimental applications.

2. Arduino Based Automatic Waterlevel Controller

An Arduino-Based Automatic Water Level Controller is an embedded system designed to monitor and automatically control the water level in a tank. It uses an Arduino microcontroller with water-level sensors to detect the tank level and automatically switch the water pump ON or OFF. The project demonstrates microcontroller programming, sensor interfacing, automation, and real-time control, making it useful for efficient water management in domestic and experimental applications.

3. Measurement of Distance using Ultrasonic Transducer With Arduino Programming

This project demonstrates the measurement of distance using an ultrasonic transducer interfaced with an Arduino microcontroller. The ultrasonic sensor emits high-frequency sound waves and detects the echo reflected from an object. The Arduino calculates the distance based on the time taken for the ultrasonic pulse to travel to the object and return to the sensor. The measured distance is then displayed through a suitable output interface. This system provides a simple, low-cost, accurate, and contactless method for distance measurement and can be applied in robotics, obstacle detection, automation, smart parking systems, and other embedded applications.
